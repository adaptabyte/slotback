HIPAA & security

Is Slotback HIPAA compliant? Yes.

Slotback was designed for protected health information from the first line of code. We sign a Business Associate Agreement on every hosted plan, and if you self-host, your patients' data never reaches us at all. Below is exactly what that means, safeguard by safeguard.

Why we don't say "HIPAA certified"

The U.S. Department of Health and Human Services does not certify software, and no official HIPAA certification exists. A practice is HIPAA compliant when its own policies, its vendors' safeguards, and Business Associate Agreements work together. Our part is to build the safeguards in, sign the BAA, and be precise about what we cover. Treat any vendor that calls itself "HIPAA certified" with suspicion.

Technical safeguards, mapped to the Security Rule

RequirementHow Slotback meets it
Unique user identification §164.312(a)(2)(i)Every staff member has an individual account with a role (admin, staff, provider). Shared logins are not possible because accounts are created by invitation for a named person.
Automatic logoff §164.312(a)(2)(iii)Sessions end after 15 minutes of inactivity and after 12 hours regardless of activity. Both limits are configurable.
Encryption and decryption §164.312(a)(2)(iv)Patient names, dates of birth, contact details, EHR identifiers and every waitlist record are encrypted field by field with AES-256-GCM before they reach the database. Each value is bound to its record, so ciphertext cannot be moved between patients. Phone numbers are looked up through keyed hashes, not plaintext.
Audit controls §164.312(b)Every sign-in, every view of a patient record, every change and every automated decision is written to an append-only audit log. Each entry includes the hash of the one before it, and the database rejects edits and deletions, so any tampering is detectable. Administrators can verify the chain from the dashboard.
Integrity §164.312(c)(1)Authenticated encryption detects any modification of stored patient data. State changes, audit entries and outgoing actions are committed in one database transaction, so a crash can't leave a booking half-made.
Person or entity authentication §164.312(d)Passwords of at least 12 characters, stored as salted scrypt hashes. Two-factor authentication with any authenticator app is required in production. Accounts lock for 15 minutes after five failed attempts. Integrations authenticate with revocable API keys, stored only as hashes.
Transmission security §164.312(e)(1)HTTPS only, with HSTS. In production Slotback refuses to start if its address or any integration endpoint is not HTTPS. Outgoing webhooks are signed so the receiver can verify them, and email requires TLS.

Web pages that show patient data also carry a strict content security policy, are never cached, never leak links through the Referer header, load no third-party scripts, and cannot be embedded in other sites.

Minimum necessary, by design

  • The matching engine works on scheduling preferences and opaque IDs. It never sees a name, phone number or diagnosis.
  • Slotback stores no diagnoses, notes or clinical content. Acuity is a number from 1 to 5; the reasoning behind it stays in your EHR.
  • Calendar feeds are read for event times and IDs only. Event titles, which often contain patient names, are never stored.
  • Writing a booking to your EHR sends only what the EHR needs to find the chart: EHR patient ID, name and date of birth.

Texts and email to patients

HIPAA permits appointment communications to patients by text and email with reasonable safeguards, and patients choose their channel and consent to texts when they join. Two message styles are available:

  • Standard: "Riverbend Health: an earlier appointment opened up: Tue, Oct 7 at 2:00 PM with Dr. Lee. Accept or decline: [link]"
  • Minimal: "Riverbend Health: You have a time-sensitive appointment update. View it securely: [link]". Recommended for behavioral health, where even the clinic's name can be sensitive. You can also set a neutral sender name.

Links are single-purpose and unguessable, expire automatically, and are stored only as hashes. They let a patient accept, decline, pause or leave, and nothing else.

The Business Associate Agreement

On every hosted plan we sign a BAA before any patient data is entered. It covers permitted uses (only providing the service to you), safeguards, subcontractors who also sign BAAs with us, breach notification without unreasonable delay and within the time HIPAA requires, your right to access and export data, and return or destruction of data when you leave.

Self-hosted practices don't need a BAA with us because we never receive or store their patients' information. They need BAAs with their own hosting, texting and email providers.

Hosted infrastructure

Each practice gets its own application instance and its own database, encrypted with its own key. Instances run on HIPAA-eligible cloud infrastructure covered by a BAA with our provider, with encrypted disks and encrypted, access-controlled backups. Our staff access production only to support you, with individual named accounts and two-factor authentication, and that access is logged. The current list of subcontractors is included with the BAA.
